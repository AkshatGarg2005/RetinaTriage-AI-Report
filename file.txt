\IEEEauthorblockA{School of Computing Science and Engineering (SCOPE), VIT Bhopal University\\Kothrikalan, Sehore, Madhya Pradesh 466114, India\\
\{akshat.23bce10641, vishwas.23bce10111, anurag.23bce10757,\\ devendra.23bce10089, tarun.23bce10695\}@vitbhopal.ac.in\\ manikandan.j@vitbhopal.ac.in}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Glaucoma causes irreversible vision loss, and in India most cases remain undiagnosed. We present RetinaTriage-AI, a two-stage screening system that crops the optic disc with an annotation-free intensity-based localiser, classifies the crop with a fine-tuned EfficientNet-B0, calibrates the output, maps it to Low, Medium and High referral bands and explains each decision with Grad-CAM++. After ONNX export the model runs on a CPU in 7.7\,ms per image. We trained the system on a widely used Kaggle collection that pools six public datasets (9,005 images) and audited the benchmark itself. MD5 hashing showed that 13.2\% of the images are byte-identical duplicates. Over five seeds, the pooled test AUC after deduplication was $0.942\pm0.003$, but the mean within-source (macro) AUC was only 0.799. A controlled four-configuration experiment of 20 training runs showed that this gap is caused by source composition: equalising source sizes reduced it from $+0.143$ to $+0.029$ (paired $t$-test, $p=0.0003$), whereas matching prevalence ($p=0.146$) or reducing data volume alone ($p=0.363$) did not. The classifier could not discriminate glaucoma on G1020, the source collected under routine clinical conditions (AUC 0.48--0.52), and it encoded the source dataset even when the source carried no label information (decodability 0.907 against a 0.229 baseline).\ifbenchmark{} Five larger CNN and transformer architectures trained on the same split raised the test AUC from \BenchOursAUC{} to at most \BenchBestAUC{} (\BenchBestModel, with 7.6 times the parameters), and none of the six models learned G1020 (AUC \BenchGMin{}--\BenchGMax).\fi{} Pooled accuracy on aggregated glaucoma benchmarks should therefore be reported together with per-source metrics and duplicate checks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Glaucoma is a progressive optic neuropathy whose damage cannot be reversed. Its global burden is projected to reach about 111.8 million people by 2040~\cite{tham2014}. In India, prevalence among adults aged 40 and above is reported at 2.7--4.3\%~\cite{rewri2023}, an estimated 11.2 million people are affected~\cite{george2010}, and roughly 90\% of cases are undiagnosed~\cite{rewri2023}. Because early disease is asymptomatic, the bottleneck is detection and prioritisation rather than treatment. Colour fundus photography is inexpensive and already used in screening camps, which makes automated analysis of fundus images an attractive way to decide which patients a specialist should see first.

Deep learning models for this task report high accuracy; a 2025 meta-analysis pooled a sensitivity of 0.92 and a specificity of 0.93, but only 35\% of the included datasets were tested externally~\cite{ling2025}. Under controlled cross-dataset transfer, AUC can fall from 0.897 to 0.580~\cite{alhasson2026}. At the same time, models are increasingly trained on large pools of public datasets: one recent model trained on 20 pooled public databases reported an internal AUROC of 0.992 against 0.875 on an external set~\cite{chaurasia2024}. How much a pooled figure reflects performance on any single source is rarely measured.

This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item A two-stage, explainable and CPU-deployable glaucoma triage pipeline that needs no segmentation masks (Section~\ref{sec:proposed}).
\item An audit of a widely used pooled Kaggle benchmark, showing that 13.2\% of its images are exact duplicates and that perceptual hashing is unsuitable for deduplicating fundus crops (Section~\ref{sec:dup}).
\item A controlled multi-seed decomposition of the gap between pooled and within-source AUC, which identifies source composition, rather than prevalence or data volume, as its cause (Section~\ref{sec:comp}).
\ifbenchmark\item A comparison with recent CNN and transformer architectures trained on the same data and split, with DeLong and McNemar significance tests (Section~\ref{sec:bench}).\fi
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Deep Learning for Fundus-Based Glaucoma Detection}
Fundus-based methods are either one-step, classifying the whole image, or two-step, first localising or segmenting the optic disc and cup~\cite{coan2023,bajwa2019}. ResNet, VGG, Inception, EfficientNet and U-Net dominate the 2019--2023 literature, and small, imbalanced datasets with inconsistent annotation are a recurring limitation~\cite{zedan2023}. Transformers have recently gained ground: a data-efficient image transformer generalised better than ResNet-50 across five external datasets~\cite{fan2023}, and on the standardised multi-source SMDG collection a vision transformer and a detection transformer reached 87.87\% and 90.48\% accuracy~\cite{chincholi2024}. MaxGlaViT, a lightweight variant of the multi-axis vision transformer, reached 92.03\% accuracy for glaucoma staging~\cite{yurdakul2025}. Alasmari \textit{et al.} segment the disc and cup with several U-Net backbones and classify on the vertical cup-to-disc ratio~\cite{alasmari2025}. The AI-GS network combines six lightweight models and reports a sensitivity of 0.935 at 95\% specificity~\cite{sharma2025}, and the RETFound foundation model~\cite{zhou2023} reached an AUC of up to 0.86 for glaucoma~\cite{chuter2025}. A regression model trained on cup-to-disc ratios generalised well across 13 data sources~\cite{hemelings2023}.

\subsection{Generalisation and Dataset Integrity}
CNNs can detect glaucoma from regions outside the optic disc~\cite{hemelings2021}, and deep networks are prone to shortcut learning~\cite{geirhos2020}. Redistributed copies of public medical datasets are common on Kaggle~\cite{jimenez2024}, and leakage from improper splitting inflates test accuracy~\cite{tampu2022}. Among glaucoma datasets, G1020 was curated to follow routine clinical practice rather than strict inclusion criteria~\cite{bajwa2020}. Table~\ref{tab:related} summarises recent work; the reported results were obtained on each study's own data and are not directly comparable with one another.

\section{Proposed Work}\label{sec:proposed}

\subsection{Dataset Description}\label{sec:data}
The data is a public Kaggle collection~\cite{kaggle} that pools six glaucoma datasets: ACRIMA~\cite{diazpinto2019}, DRISHTI-GS~\cite{sivaswamy2014}, G1020~\cite{bajwa2020}, LAG~\cite{li2019}, ORIGA~\cite{zhang2010} and RIM-ONE~\cite{batista2020}. It contains 9,005 labelled colour fundus photographs, 5,624 normal and 3,381 glaucomatous (37.5\% positive), collected in five countries with different cameras and inclusion criteria (Table~\ref{tab:data}, Fig.~\ref{fig:data}). Prevalence ranges from 25.8\% (ORIGA) to 69.3\% (DRISHTI-GS). ACRIMA images are distributed already cropped around the optic disc, whereas G1020 contains full-field photographs taken in routine practice. Each image is labelled from the name of its own folder, and its source dataset is recorded so that performance can be measured per source. LAG supplies 53.9\% of the images, rising to 62.1\% once the duplicates described in Section~\ref{sec:dup} are removed.

\subsection{Stage 1: Optic Disc Localisation}
The optic disc is usually the brightest coherent structure in a well-exposed fundus photograph, so no annotated masks are needed. The image is converted to greyscale and smoothed with a $51\times51$ Gaussian kernel to suppress small highlights, and the location of maximum intensity is taken as the disc centre. A square window with a side of 45\% of the shorter image axis is cropped around it. Near a border, the window is shifted inward rather than clipped, so every crop stays square. Contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalisation (CLAHE, clip limit 2.0, $8\times8$ tiles)~\cite{zuiderveld1994} is applied to the green channel, which carries the highest vessel and rim contrast. Each crop is flagged \texttt{edge\_hit} if its centre lies within 10\% of the border, and \texttt{low\_contrast} if it is barely brighter than the whole image. Of the 9,005 crops, 91.0\% were clean, 4.6\% were edge hits and 4.4\% were low-contrast (Fig.~\ref{fig:roi}).

\subsection{Stage 2: Classifier}
The classifier is an EfficientNet-B0~\cite{tan2019} initialised with ImageNet weights. Blocks 0--4 are frozen and blocks 5 onward are fine-tuned, giving 3.70\,M trainable parameters out of 4.01\,M. The head is dropout ($p=0.4$) followed by a two-class linear layer. Training uses a class-weighted cross-entropy loss with weights computed from the training split and label smoothing of 0.1, Adam with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$ and a batch size of 32, and augmentation on the training split only (horizontal flip, rotation of $\pm10^\circ$, brightness and contrast jitter of 0.15). Training stops when the validation AUC has not improved for four epochs (at most 12 epochs), and the best checkpoint is restored. At inference, predictions are averaged over the image and its horizontal mirror.

\subsection{Calibration and Triage}
Class weighting and label smoothing distort the output distribution, so a temperature $T$ is fitted on the validation set and the logits are divided by it~\cite{guo2017}. This changes the probabilities without changing their ranking. Both band boundaries are derived from the validation ROC curve, never from the test set. The Low/Medium boundary is the highest threshold that still achieves 95\% sensitivity, so few glaucoma cases are placed in the Low band. The Medium/High boundary is the threshold at which specificity reaches 95\%, so urgent referrals are rarely false alarms.

\subsection{Explainability}
Grad-CAM++~\cite{chattopadhay2018} weights the activation maps of the last convolutional block by higher-order gradients of the glaucoma output. Heatmaps are computed on exactly the image that is displayed, so the overlay is spatially aligned. The final feature map is $7\times7$, so a heatmap indicates a region rather than individual rim features.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
All metrics are computed on a held-out test split with un-augmented images. Discrimination is measured by the ROC AUC and by sensitivity at 95\% specificity. The \emph{macro AUC} is the unweighted mean of per-source AUCs over sources with at least 30 test images, which excludes DRISHTI-GS. The \emph{gap} is the pooled AUC minus the macro AUC. Duplicates are identified by MD5 hashes of the original files. A logistic-regression probe with three-fold cross-validation measures whether the source dataset can be decoded from the 1,280-dimensional penultimate embeddings, compared with the majority-class baseline.

To find what drives the gap, four configurations (Table~\ref{tab:configs}) were each trained with five seeds (42, 1, 7, 13 and 99). The seed controls the split, any subsampling and the weight initialisation. Configurations are compared with paired $t$-tests across seeds. \ifbenchmark For the architecture comparison, five further ImageNet-pretrained models, ResNet-50~\cite{he2016}, DenseNet-121~\cite{huang2017}, ConvNeXt-T~\cite{liu2022convnext}, Swin-T~\cite{liu2021swin} and MaxViT-T~\cite{tu2022maxvit}, were fully fine-tuned with a new two-class linear layer, using the loss, optimiser, augmentation and stopping rule above, while EfficientNet-B0 kept the configuration of Section~\ref{sec:proposed}. They are compared with ours on the same test images using the DeLong test for AUC~\cite{delong1988,sun2014} and the exact McNemar test for paired classifications~\cite{mcnemar1947}.\fi{} All models were implemented in PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019} and scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011} and trained on an Apple M2 with the MPS backend.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Duplicate Audit}\label{sec:dup}
MD5 hashing found 2,380 byte-identical images forming 1,190 pairs, all within ACRIMA and RIM-ONE: each source appears exactly twice in the collection (Table~\ref{tab:dup}). No pair had conflicting labels. Overall, 13.2\% of the collection is redundant, so a random split places identical images in both the training and the test set. A perceptual hash (64-bit DCT hash, Hamming distance $\le 4$) flagged 26.3\% of the images, but at least 4.4\% of its groups mixed normal and glaucoma labels, which is impossible for true duplicates, and visual inspection confirmed that it grouped different eyes. Fundus crops share a common structure (a bright disc on a darker field with radiating vessels) that a low-frequency hash cannot separate, so only the MD5 duplicates were removed.

\subsection{Pooled versus Within-Source Performance}\label{sec:comp}
Table~\ref{tab:multiseed} and Fig.~\ref{fig:comp} report the four configurations, and Table~\ref{tab:ttests} gives the paired tests. On the deduplicated pool (A), the pooled AUC of $0.942\pm0.003$ exceeded the macro AUC by $+0.143\pm0.014$. Matching prevalence across sources (B) left the gap statistically unchanged ($p=0.146$), so the model was not exploiting differences in base rates. Equalising source sizes (C) reduced the gap to $+0.029$ on every seed ($p=0.0003$). Configuration D, which has the same volume as C but the original composition, kept a gap of $+0.125$, indistinguishable from A ($p=0.363$), and equalising sizes at that volume removed it ($p=0.0085$). From A to C, the pooled AUC fell by 0.109 while the macro AUC rose by 0.005: the composition changed, the model did not get worse. LAG makes up about 62\% of the test set in A, B and D and scores near 0.98, so it dominates the sample-weighted pooled AUC; across all 20 runs the gap correlated with LAG's share of the test set ($r=0.856$). The relationship is not strictly monotonic (B has a higher LAG share than A but a slightly smaller gap), so the evidence supports the effect of equalising sizes rather than a linear dose--response.

\subsection{Per-Source Behaviour and Source Encoding}
Table~\ref{tab:persource} gives the per-source AUC averaged over seeds. ACRIMA and LAG stayed near 0.98 in every configuration, while G1020 stayed at chance (0.48--0.52 across 15 training runs). No change in composition made G1020 learnable for this pipeline. The source dataset remained highly decodable from the classifier's embeddings: in C, where an image's source carries no information about its label, the probe still reached $0.907\pm0.006$ against a baseline of 0.229. An earlier hypothesis, that localisation failures explained the weak sources, was rejected in a prototype run: ACRIMA had the second-highest rate of flagged crops (22.7\%) but the highest AUC, and restricting the test set to clean crops changed the AUC only from 0.961 to 0.966.

\subsection{Explainability and Deployment}
Fig.~\ref{fig:gradcam} shows Grad-CAM++ explanations from the prototype checkpoint. \section{Discussion}
On a pooled multi-source benchmark, a pooled AUC mostly reports performance on the largest and easiest constituent dataset. In our data that source is LAG; equalising source sizes removed the gap, whereas matching prevalence and reducing volume did not. Exact duplicates add a second, independent inflation. The failure on G1020 is the result most relevant to deployment, because G1020 is the source closest to routine practice.

\ifbenchmark
The architecture comparison supports this reading. Replacing EfficientNet-B0 with models of 1.7 to 7.6 times as many parameters raised the pooled AUC by at most 0.012, and the DeLong test found the gain significant only for ConvNeXt-T, Swin-T and MaxViT-T ($p$ between 0.019 and 0.045). LAG alone makes up 62.1\% of the deduplicated pool, and all six models exceeded 0.98 AUC on it, so a pooled gain of this size says little about the difficult sources: no model reached 0.61 on G1020, and on ORIGA all six stayed between 0.75 and 0.82. At the fixed threshold of 0.5, EfficientNet-B0 had the highest recall (0.877) and the lowest specificity (0.828). Its significant McNemar results come from a higher false-positive count (129, against 33 to 92 for the other models), partly offset by fewer missed glaucoma cases (52, against 53 to 98). RetinaTriage-AI does not use the 0.5 threshold; its bands are set from the validation ROC, and at 95\% specificity its sensitivity (0.763) lay within the range of the other models (0.720 to 0.803). For a screening tool that has to run on a CPU, the smaller model is therefore a reasonable choice, and on this benchmark the limiting factor is the data rather than the architecture.
\fi

The work has limitations. All estimates are internal, from random splits of public research data, and are not a cross-site validation. The composition experiments used a single architecture\ifbenchmark{}. The architecture comparison used a single seed, fine-tuned the comparison models fully while EfficientNet-B0 kept five blocks frozen, and computed threshold-dependent metrics from uncalibrated scores\fi. DRISHTI-GS is too small for per-source estimates. Cropping to the optic disc discards peripapillary information that CNNs can use~\cite{hemelings2021}, and the $7\times7$ Grad-CAM++ map is too coarse to show rim features. The system has not been clinically validated.

% ============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
RetinaTriage-AI turns a fundus photograph into a calibrated referral band with a visual explanation, and it runs on a CPU. Evaluating it carefully showed that a widely used pooled benchmark contains 13.2\% exact duplicates and that its pooled AUC overstates within-source performance by about 0.14 because of source composition. \ifbenchmark{}Five larger CNN and transformer architectures trained on the same data improved the pooled AUC by at most 0.012 and did not make G1020 learnable, which places the bottleneck in the data rather than the model. \fi{}We recommend that results on aggregated glaucoma benchmarks report per-source and macro metrics, check for duplicates and control for composition. Future work will evaluate leave-one-source-out generalisation, diagnose the G1020 failure, replicate the analysis on the standardised SMDG collection~\cite{kiefer2023}, and deploy the single-file ONNX model as a privacy-preserving web service.
